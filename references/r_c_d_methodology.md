# R→C→D Methodology: Reasoning → Critique → Decision

This document explains the Reasoning → Critique → Decision (R→C→D) model, a core component of the Feature Request Framework. R→C→D is a structured thinking process designed to improve the quality of decisions by forcing a deliberate and critical examination of the reasoning behind them.

## When to Use R→C→D

The framework specifies when to use R→C→D:

- **🔴 MANDATORY**: For steps that are highly prone to error if not carefully considered. These are:
    - **Phase 1.2**: Separating symptoms from the root problem.
    - **Phase 4.1**: Defining success criteria.
    - **Phase 4.2**: Identifying the primary bottleneck.
- **🟡 IF COMPLEX**: For steps that have a high degree of uncertainty or involve multiple interacting parts. This applies to:
    - **Phase 2.3**: Classifying the Techstack Track for a feature request.

## The Three Stages of R→C→D

### 1. Reasoning
This is the stage where you lay out your thinking. The goal is to make your reasoning explicit and transparent.

- **Objective**: What are you trying to achieve in this step?
- **Input**: What data and information are you working with?
- **Knowns vs. Assumptions**: What are the confirmed facts, and what are the unverified assumptions?
- **Alternative Interpretations**: What are the different ways this information could be interpreted? Propose 2-3 different options.
- **Comparison**: How do the different interpretations compare? What are the pros and cons of each?

### 2. Critique
This is the stage where you challenge your own reasoning. The goal is to identify potential flaws, biases, or blind spots.

- **Challenge Assumptions**: Which of the core assumptions could be wrong? What would be the impact if they were?
- **Identify Blind Spots**: Are there any alternative interpretations that have been missed? Are you falling into any common cognitive biases (e.g., confirmation bias)?
- **Validate Data**: Is the data sufficient to support the reasoning? Is it reliable? Could it be misleading?

### 3. Decision
This is the stage where you make a final, well-justified decision.

- **Conclusion**: State your decision clearly and concisely.
- **Justification**: Explain the evidence and reasoning that support your decision.
- **Rejected Alternatives**: Briefly explain why other alternatives were not chosen.
- **Open Assumptions**: List any remaining assumptions that still need to be validated.

By following this structured process, teams can make more robust, evidence-based decisions and reduce the risk of costly mistakes.
